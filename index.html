Introduction

The deep sea, from 1000 to 11000 metres, is approximately 95% unexplored (Li et al., 2023). With high pressures, freezing temperatures and no sunlight, it presents some of the most extreme conditions for life to survive. Despite these challenges, snailfish octopuses and other sea creatures have adapted to thrive in these depths. This article raises the question of whether engineers can replicate these natural adaptations with bioinspired soft robots. The following sections describe how these robots move, how they can withstand pressure, what they have achieved and what still limits them.
